# Variant 8 – HR Leave
## Customer Discovery Questions (Mom Test)

### Business problem

HR receives many employee questions about leave. The objective is to understand how HR currently handles these questions, which problems occur frequently, and whether an AI assistant could genuinely help.

### 1. Understand the current process

**Q1.** Can you walk me through the most recent time an employee contacted HR with a question about leave?

*Purpose:* Understand the actual process instead of asking how the process should work.

**Q2.** What are the most common types of leave-related questions employees have asked your team recently?

*Purpose:* Identify recurring topics such as annual leave, sick leave, parental leave and carry-over.

**Q3.** How do employees usually contact HR when they have leave-related questions?

*Purpose:* Understand the existing communication channels, such as email, phone, internal portals or messaging platforms.

### 2. Understand workload and challenges

**Q4.** Approximately how many leave-related questions does your team receive in a normal week or month?

*Purpose:* Establish the actual volume and frequency of requests rather than assuming there are hundreds.

**Q5.** Which types of leave questions take the most time for your team to resolve, and why?

*Purpose:* Identify time-consuming questions and potential automation opportunities.

**Q6.** Can you describe a recent leave-related question that was difficult to answer or required repeated follow-ups?

*Purpose:* Discover real examples of complexity, missing information or communication problems.

### 3. Understand information and policy management

**Q7.** Where does your team currently find the information needed to answer employee leave questions?

*Purpose:* Understand where policy information is stored, such as documents, spreadsheets, HR systems or internal portals.

**Q8.** How does your team handle questions when a leave policy is unclear, outdated or does not cover an employee's situation?

*Purpose:* Understand policy gaps and how HR handles exceptions.

**Q9.** How do employees and HR know whether they are referring to the latest approved leave policy?

*Purpose:* Understand policy updates, version control and the risk of providing outdated information.

### 4. Understand approvals, risks and expectations

**Q10.** Can you describe a recent leave request that needed additional review or approval? What happened?

*Purpose:* Understand which activities require human judgement and manager or HR involvement.

**Q11.** What types of employee information or leave-related questions should only be accessible to authorised HR staff?

*Purpose:* Identify privacy, confidentiality and access-control requirements.

**Q12.** How does your team currently measure whether leave-related employee queries are being handled successfully?

*Purpose:* Understand existing success measures, such as response time, resolution time, repeated queries and employee satisfaction.

---

## Interview guidance – The Mom Test

During the discussion:

1. Ask about actual past experiences instead of hypothetical future situations.
2. Request specific examples when an answer is general.
3. Avoid asking whether the stakeholder likes the idea of an AI assistant.
4. Do not assume that every leave question is suitable for automation.
5. Listen for problems that occur repeatedly and take significant time to resolve.
6. Understand which questions require HR judgement, approval or escalation.

### What we want to learn

By the end of the discovery conversation, we should understand:

- The volume and types of leave questions HR receives.
- The current process and communication channels.
- Which questions are repetitive and time-consuming.
- Where HR gets its policy information.
- How policy updates and exceptions are handled.
- Which decisions require human intervention.
- What privacy and access controls are necessary.
- How the organisation measures successful resolution.

### Expected discovery outcome

Use the interview findings to determine whether a policy-based AI assistant could answer routine leave questions using approved HR documents.

The assistant should not independently approve leave, make employment decisions or invent policy entitlements. Questions involving exceptions, unclear policies or sensitive employee matters should be escalated to authorised HR staff.
